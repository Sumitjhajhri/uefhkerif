# Meridian Rail — Staff Handbook (extract)

## Rostering

Rosters are published fourteen days in advance. A published roster may be
changed with less than seventy-two hours' notice only by agreement with the
member of staff affected, except in the case of a major disruption, when the
duty controller may reassign staff within their existing shift.

No member of operational staff may work more than nine consecutive hours on
duty, or more than six consecutive days without a rest day. A minimum rest
period of twelve hours applies between the end of one duty and the start of
the next.

Shift swaps between staff of the same grade are permitted with supervisor
approval, provided the rest-period rule is not breached by either party.

## Training and competence

All operational staff hold a competence certificate renewed every two years.
A member of staff whose certificate has lapsed may not work in an operational
role until reassessment is complete. Reassessment is booked through the
training office at Callow Bridge and must be offered within ten working days
of a request.

Ticket inspectors complete an additional module covering the penalty fare
provisions and the handling of disputes. The module is repeated annually.

New operational staff work under supervision for their first twenty duties.
A supervising member of staff may oversee no more than two trainees at once.

## Uniform and identification

Operational staff in public-facing roles wear the issued uniform and display
a photographic identity card at all times on duty. A member of staff who
cannot produce their identity card may not carry out ticket inspection.

Replacement identity cards are issued by the training office. A lost card
must be reported the same day.

## Reporting concerns

A member of staff who believes a safety rule has been breached reports it to
the duty controller immediately and records it in the safety log before the
end of their duty. Reports may be made without attribution. No member of staff
may be disciplined for making a report in good faith, including where the
concern is later found to be unfounded.
